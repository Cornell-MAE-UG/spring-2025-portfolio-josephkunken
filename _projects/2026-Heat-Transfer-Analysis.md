This project involved a multi-layer thermal analysis of a photovoltaic (PV) panel to determine solar-to-electrical conversion efficiency. The system accounted for the absorption, reflection, and transmission of solar radiation across composite material layers, including glass, adhesive, and silicon.  


To evaluate the system, I developed a thermal resistance network to determine the steady-state temperatures at each material boundary. This model integrated several environmental and material variables, including:
- Environmental Factors
    - Incident solar radiation
    - Ambient air temperature
    - Convective heat transfer coefficients (h)
- Material Properties
    - Layer thicknesses
    - Thermal conductivity coefficients (k) for each material

By solving the temperature profiles, I was able to quantify the net electrical power output and overall efficacy of the panel under specific climate conditions. Such analysis can then be used to identify the feasibility of the electrical production from such a PV panel.  This analytical framework is directly transferable to other thermal management challenges, such as heat dissipation in data centers or in microprocessors.
